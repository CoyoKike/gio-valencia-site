Boston doesn't need more promises. It needs a plan, drawn with the people who live here, and the patience to build it block by block, in every neighborhood.

Triple-deckers, Dorchester

The plan

  1. 01

    Homes people can afford

    More homes near transit, faster permits for affordable buildings, and real protection for the tenants already here.

  2. 02

    Streets that work

    Buses that come on time, sidewalks you can push a stroller on, and crossings that are safe for kids and seniors.

  3. 03

    Neighborhoods plan first

    Residents at the table before the drawings are done, not after. Plans in every language spoken on the block.

  4. 04

    A council that shows up

    Office hours in all 23 neighborhoods, every vote explained in plain words, and the city's data open to everyone.

About Gio

He draws the plan. Then he organizes the block to build it.

Gio Valencia is an architect and urban planner who has spent his career on Boston's streets: drawing buildings, planning neighborhoods and organizing the residents who live in them.

He has knocked on doors, run the community meetings and sat across the table from legislators at the State House to turn what neighbors ask for into law. On the council, he will do the same for the whole city.
